# Choose a session and leave with a next step

Write one question you want the conference to help answer. Choose a session,
prepare one source, and decide what you will do with the answer.

## Before choosing

Use the [planning guide](planning-guide.md) for goals, availability, alternatives,
and bookings. Check [times and places](time-and-place.md) for entrances and room
gaps. The [schedule](../exports/schedule.md) is a dated snapshot; confirm changes
on the official event page.

For another event, try the [worked event comparison](../resources/stay-current/event-comparison.md),
[announced conferences](../resources/stay-current/conferences.md),
and [participation conditions](../resources/stay-current/opportunities.md).
Keep ticket and workshop charges separate. Record currency, pricing date,
eligibility, travel assumptions, and access needs before choosing.

## A worked learning path

**Example, not an attendance record.** A developer wants to understand recovery.
They open the [worker reading guide](../resources/related-talks/talks-lessons-from-building-a-background-task-processing-library.md),
read the linked Tokio shutdown documentation, and write:

> If a worker stops after an external effect but before recording completion,
> what evidence tells the next worker whether to retry?

They compare the session with its same-time alternative, check access and
availability, and keep the question in the [session template](../templates/session.md).
After attending or studying a source, they can record what changed and design
a small experiment. Its result remains unknown until they run it.

## If you cannot attend

Look for published material in the [archives](../resources/conferences/README.md).
Check whether a link is a recording, abstract, slides, or code. A recording link
does not establish playback, captions, or permission to reuse it.

For the worker example, a remote learner can start with the Tokio documentation,
write the same question, and inspect the linked project documentation. If no talk
recording is available, these sources still support study; they do not reproduce
what the speaker said. Text also offers an option when bandwidth is limited.

Record participation context, source, interpretation, and next action. Add observed
results when available. Adapt a [reading or experiment session](../resources/stay-current/participate.md#learn-together)
to learn with others. Publish conversation notes only with permission.
