## Start with a question

For example: *How should a Rust worker stop without losing accepted work?*
Open the [background-worker reading list](../resources/related-talks/talks-lessons-from-building-a-background-task-processing-library.md).
Use its Tokio shutdown guide as preparation, compare the linked worker project,
then record what you still need to test. The [proposal example](../resources/speaking/worker-talk-example.md)
shows how that question could become a talk.

For another interest, browse [topics](../resources/topics/README.md),
[speakers' work](../resources/speakers/README.md), or [my learning goals](../my-conference/README.md).

## Know what you are reading

Official pages establish published event facts. Topic connections are editorial
judgments. Examples are labelled; observations require an actual report.
Check source dates, access conditions, and stated coverage before deciding.
An older program remains useful even when its recording is unavailable.

[Suggest a correction or resource](../CONTRIBUTING.md). A source link and the
question it helps answer are enough to start.
