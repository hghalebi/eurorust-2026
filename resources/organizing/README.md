# Plan a Rust event people can use

Begin with the audience and what they should leave able to do. Adapt the scope
to a meetup, workshop, online discussion, or conference.

Copy the [event plan](../../templates/event-plan.md). The
[worked discussion example](example-event.md) is a planning exercise with explicit
assumptions; no event has been announced.

## Define the event

Agree on outcomes, format, roles, dates, and spending limits. Give each decision
an owner. Estimates need a currency, date, source, and contingency assumption.
Replace estimates with confirmed terms when available.

The [RustBridge before-event guide](https://rustbridge.com/organizing/before/)
is a practical workshop reference. Rust's [community page](https://rust-lang.org/community/)
offers an event-organizing support route; specific support needs confirmation.

## Build a program

State audience, formats, duration, prerequisites, selection criteria, deadline
and timezone, support, and material-sharing terms in the call. Explain review
conflicts of interest and how decisions are communicated.

Use the [speaking guide](../speaking/README.md) for preparation and the
[speaker research template](../../templates/speaker-research.md) to connect
potential speakers with relevant public work. An archive profile does
not establish availability. Check room, speaker, setup, and travel constraints.
Publish schedule changes with a version and explanation;
[pretalx documents](https://docs.pretalx.org/user/schedule/) one approach.

## Make participation practical

Share venue, joining, and access details through appropriate public or registered
participant channels; follow the event's disclosure policy. Explain language,
captioning, costs, and help routes. Distinguish watching, interacting, and replays.
State unknowns. Link the event's conduct and private reporting routes.

Confirm workshop equipment with the [readiness checklist](../../templates/workshop-readiness.md).
Define volunteer roles, handovers, and breaks. Keep private operational contacts
and incident records outside public documents.

## Prepare the day

Use a run sheet: time, activity, room, owner, equipment, fallback, communication.
Rehearse presentation setup. Agree recording, photography, caption, and publication
permissions beforehand. Verify local venue, legal, safety, and access requirements
with their responsible owners; a generic checklist cannot confirm them.

## Preserve the work

Track session IDs, speaker credit, slides, code, recording, captions/transcript
status, permissions, sources, and publication status. Keep stable links and mark
unavailable items. Check playback and access separately from successful upload.

## Improve the next edition

Use the [retrospective](../../templates/event-retrospective.md): decision, constraint,
observed outcome, evidence, and next change. Voluntary feedback does not represent
everyone. Publish actual figures only with permission and context.

For examples, compare [RustFest's budget explanation](https://rustfest.global/information/the-numbers/)
with the Rust Project Program team's [All Hands 2026 retrospective](https://blog.rust-lang.org/inside-rust/2026/07/31/all-hands-2026-retrospective/).
The former includes projections; the latter reports a particular team gathering.
Neither establishes a budget or result for your event.
